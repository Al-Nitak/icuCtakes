Regarding this patient, he was admitted after fourth ventricle tumor resection for monitoring in the surgical ICU. The patient has a medical history including benign testicular tumor resection nine years ago, ureteral stone removal six months ago, gastritis, and chronic cervical spine stenosis at C5-C7. Upon ICU admission, his level of consciousness was approximately 12 out of 15, and he had a tongue laceration due to prone positioning during surgery. Oral and maxillofacial surgery was consulted and prescribed lidocaine gel and dexamethasone. He was complaining of severe headache and neck pain, so a CT scan was performed upon ICU admission. The CT showed residual tumor and subdural hemorrhage, but no midline shift, hydrocephalus, evidence of brain herniation, or acute regional infarction. The patient was hemodynamically stable and breathing room air with oxygen saturation 98%. We started him on clear liquid diet then advanced to regular diet as tolerated. He was receiving normal saline at 80 ml per hour, and today is his third day, so we stopped this infusion and continued only with oral diet. He has good urine output of 100 to 150 ml per hour. We started prophylactic antibiotics from surgery and continued them for three days. The patient is now receiving analgesics including tramadol 50 mg every 8 hours, paracetamol every 8 hours, and Zivo 8 mg every 12 hours. We started hypertension management today.
